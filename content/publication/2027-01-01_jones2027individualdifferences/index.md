title = "Individual differences in search efficiency are not predictive of subclinical psychopathological traits"
date = "0001-01-01"
publication_types = [ "1" ]
publication = "_Under Submission_"
abstract = "The ability to search complex problem spaces efficiently is a hallmark of intelligent behaviour, yet how search varies between people, and whether that variation is systematically associated with sub-clinical psychopathological traits, remains relatively underexplored. To address this, N = 203 adults completed a generative search task known as Zendo, in which the aim is to discover the hidden rules governing visual arrays of cones of varied size and colour by constructing and testing novel arrays. Two-hundred participants also completed the 53-item Brief Symptom Inventory, which measures anxiety, depression, hostility, interpersonal sensitivity, obsessive-compulsive behaviour, paranoid ideation, phobic anxiety, psychoticism, and somatisation. Analyses reveal significant individual differences in generative search behaviour that meaningfully shape learning outcomes. Individuals tailored their search to the structure of the problem space in light of the feedback that they elicited, and those who elicited a balance of positive and negative feedback narrowed the problem space most effectively and showed a learning advantage at test. Although we report reliable variation in sub-clinical psychopathological traits, converging analyses, including Bayes factor analysis and equivalence tests, provide positive evidence for a true null at the intersection of psychopathological traits and generative search behaviour, which is bounded at most to a small-to-moderate effect for a reliable measure of search efficiency. Overall, people show significant individual differences in their ability to search complex problem spaces efficiently, though this variation is not reliably associated with sub-clinical psychopathology."
